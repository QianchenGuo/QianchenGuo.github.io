In the 18th century, it was common practice to solve differential equations using [integral transforms](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=599857471&content_type=Answer&match_order=1&q=%E7%A7%AF%E5%88%86%E5%8F%98%E6%8D%A2&zhida_source=entity).

Euler was the first to derive ∫X(x)eᵃˣdx, Lagrange followed with ∫X(x)e⁻ᵃˣaˣdx, and Laplace introduced ∫xˢφ(x)dx in 1785. At the time, however, there was no notion of function spaces—these tools were applied to differential equations naively, without deeper reflection.

It was only after Fourier proposed his transform in the early 19th century that Laplace came to reassess his earlier work. Later, it became clear that *s* could be interpreted as an extension of frequency into the complex domain.
