Let us assume an [LTI system](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=734237849&content_type=Answer&match_order=1&q=LTI%E7%B3%BB%E7%BB%9F&zhida_source=entity) and refer to the combined D/A–A/D setup as a sampled-data system. Naturally, sampling implies a loss of information, and [controllability/observability](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=734237849&content_type=Answer&match_order=1&q=%E8%83%BD%E6%8E%A7%E8%83%BD%E8%A7%82%E6%80%A7&zhida_source=entity) may not be preserved.

Regarding the preservation of controllability and observability, a key concept here is **[pathological sampling](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=734237849&content_type=Answer&match_order=1&q=%E7%97%85%E6%80%81%E9%87%87%E6%A0%B7&zhida_source=entity)**.

**Definition**: Pathological sampling is sampling that destroys the controllability/observability of the original system. For an LTI system $\dot x=Ax$ with sampling angular frequency $\omega _{s}$, if $\exists k\in \mathbb Z$ and a pair of eigenvalues $\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}$ such that $Re(\lambda _{1})=Re(\lambda _{2}) \\ \left| Im(\lambda _{1})-Im(\lambda _{2}) \right|=k\omega_{s}$, then the sampling angular frequency $\omega _{s}$ is pathological.

In essence, this means there exist poles with equal real parts whose imaginary parts differ by an integer multiple of the sampling frequency. What makes pathological sampling intriguing is that a shorter sampling interval is not always better. Sometimes a small sampling interval can lead to divergence due to its impact on controllability/observability, yet this divergence does not imply that the controller has failed.
