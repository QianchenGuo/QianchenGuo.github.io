Because innovation is the wellspring of development.

The development of mathematics in China has experienced twists and turns and faced many hardships, and it still finds itself in a special period today, with considerable room for improvement.

Whether in the domestic research environment or the educational one, there is a greater inclination toward producing more problem-solvers and exam-takers. Undeniably, this approach can still yield some work in the field of mathematics, but if the goal is to become a great mathematician, such efforts rarely stand out. A look at the reasons behind Fields Medal recipients' awards reveals that nearly all winners have made groundbreaking, pioneering contributions in a specific area, or have solved a uniquely valuable problem. [Terence Tao](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=587737884&content_type=Answer&match_order=1&q=%E9%99%B6%E5%93%B2%E8%BD%A9&zhida_source=entity) is one of the few who won for simultaneously making significant contributions across multiple fields that are not closely related, yet even Tao belongs to the category of those who can pose their own problems and drive innovation.

I believe Professor Yau's remarks are quite urgent, aiming to appeal to students to correctly recognize the key to doing good mathematics—the key to doing "great" mathematics—and also hoping that Chinese mathematics can embrace more innovation and achieve better development.
