## I. The Complete [Technology Stack](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E6%8A%80%E6%9C%AF%E6%A0%88&zhida_source=entity) of Classical Manipulation

A typical classical system is not "RRT generates a path, then execute," but rather:

$$
[ \text{Task logic} \rightarrow \text{Contact mode/grasp mode} \rightarrow \text{Geometric path} \rightarrow \text{Dynamics and time parameterization} \rightarrow \text{Task-space/whole-body control} \rightarrow \text{Force and impedance control} \rightarrow \text{Real contact} ]
$$

Here, RRT/[PRM](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=PRM&zhida_source=entity) belongs only to the third layer, and is merely one tool within it.

From an [academic lineage](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%AD%A6%E6%9C%AF%E8%B0%B1%E7%B3%BB&zhida_source=entity) perspective, at least the following eight core research lines exist.

## II. Geometric Motion Planning: From [Configuration Space](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E6%9E%84%E5%9E%8B%E7%A9%BA%E9%97%B4&zhida_source=entity) to Trajectory Optimization

### 1. Configuration Space and Exact Planning

The foundation of modern [motion planning](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=motion+planning&zhida_source=entity) is not sampling, but **[configuration space](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=configuration+space&zhida_source=entity)**: representing each robot configuration as $q\in\mathcal C$; transforming physical collisions into obstacle sets in $\mathcal C$; and reducing the planning problem to finding a connected curve in $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{free}}$.

Lozano-Pérez's 1983 configuration-space paper is one of the most important foundational works for [geometric planning](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%87%A0%E4%BD%95%E8%A7%84%E5%88%92&zhida_source=entity) of manipulators. Latombe's *Robot Motion Planning* systematically summarizes classical methods such as cell decomposition, roadmap, potential field, reachability, and complexity. ([IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1676196/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

This period explored many methods that are rarely mentioned later:

-   Exact and approximate cell decomposition;
-   Generalized Voronoi diagrams;
-   Visibility graphs;
-   Algebraic/semi-algebraic motion planning;
-   Navigation functions;
-   Analytic or [discretized](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E7%A6%BB%E6%95%A3%E5%8C%96&zhida_source=entity) C-space obstacle construction.

These methods are not "brute-force sampling," but they often suffer from the [curse of dimensionality](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E7%BB%B4%E6%95%B0%E7%81%BE%E9%9A%BE&zhida_source=entity) on high-DOF manipulators.

### 2. [Potential Fields](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%8A%BF%E5%9C%BA&zhida_source=entity) and the Unification of Planning and Control

Khatib's 1986 artificial potential field method is not merely a simple "attractive plus [repulsive](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E6%8E%92%E6%96%A5%E5%8A%9B&zhida_source=entity) force" algorithm. Its significance lies in writing obstacle avoidance directly into the real-time control law, thereby beginning the fusion of motion planning and feedback control. Rimon and Koditschek subsequently developed navigation functions with rigorous properties, avoiding spurious local minima of ordinary potential fields under specific [topological](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E6%8B%93%E6%89%91&zhida_source=entity) conditions. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/027836498600500106?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

This line later influenced:

-   Reactive motion generation;
-   Whole-body collision avoidance;
-   Control barrier functions;
-   Obstacle cost terms in differential dynamic programming;
-   Operational-space obstacle avoidance.

### 3. PRM and RRT as Compromises under High-Dimensional Difficulty

The representative work on PRM is Kavraki, Švestka, Latombe, and Overmars 1996; RRT-Connect is Kuffner and LaValle 2000. Their contribution is to circumvent the difficulty of explicitly constructing high-dimensional free space via [probabilistic completeness](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E6%A6%82%E7%8E%87%E5%AE%8C%E5%A4%87%E6%80%A7&zhida_source=entity) or fast [space exploration](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E7%A9%BA%E9%97%B4%E6%8E%A2%E7%B4%A2&zhida_source=entity). ([Kavraki Lab](https://www.kavrakilab.org/publications/kavraki-svestka1996probabilistic-roadmaps-for.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Thus, sampling-based planning should be understood as an engineering compromise formed after [classical geometric](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E7%BB%8F%E5%85%B8%E5%87%A0%E4%BD%95&zhida_source=entity) planning became computationally intractable in high-dimensional spaces—not as classical planning theory itself.

A crucial distinction must be made: **probabilistic completeness is a search property, not execution robustness.** The fact that a planner will eventually find a path with high probability does not mean the robot can still complete the task under friction variations, object shifts, or contact impacts.

### 4. Trajectory Optimization

After 2009, a body of work began shifting from "finding a collision-free path" to "directly optimizing the entire trajectory":

-   CHOMP: covariant gradient optimization in the [function space](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%87%BD%E6%95%B0%E7%A9%BA%E9%97%B4&zhida_source=entity) of trajectories;
-   STOMP: gradient-free optimization via stochastic trajectory perturbations;
-   TrajOpt: improving efficiency via sequential convex optimization and continuous collision constraints. ([RI Publications](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/chomp-gradient-optimization-techniques-for-efficient-motion-planning?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

These methods are also the direct ancestors of much of today's robot policy [post-processing](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%90%8E%E5%A4%84%E7%90%86&zhida_source=entity), trajectory refinement, and diffusion trajectory optimization.

## III. Redundant [Kinematics](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E8%BF%90%E5%8A%A8%E5%AD%A6&zhida_source=entity), Task-Space Control, and Action Generation

This line is, in fact, the "core [control theory](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E6%8E%A7%E5%88%B6%E7%90%86%E8%AE%BA&zhida_source=entity)" of manipulation that most closely resembles locomotion.

### 1. [Redundant Manipulators](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%86%97%E4%BD%99%E6%9C%BA%E6%A2%B0%E8%87%82&zhida_source=entity) and the Null Space

Liégeois 1977 proposed using the Jacobian null space to optimize secondary objectives while keeping the primary task unchanged:

$$
\dot q = J^\dagger \dot x + \left(I-J^\dagger J\right)z .
$$

The second term can be used to:

-   Stay away from joint limits;
-   Avoid [singular configurations](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%A5%87%E5%BC%82%E4%BD%8D%E5%BD%A2&zhida_source=entity);
-   Improve manipulability;
-   Adjust posture;
-   Avoid obstacles;
-   Optimize contact forces.

Nakamura and Hanafusa subsequently studied singularity-robust [inverse kinematics](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E9%80%86%E8%BF%90%E5%8A%A8%E5%AD%A6&zhida_source=entity); Siciliano and Slotine further developed a rigorous multi-task priority framework. ([IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel5/21/4309635/04309644.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)). This is not ordinary inverse kinematics, but rather the theoretical starting point for all that followed: whole-body control; task-priority control; stack of tasks; null-space posture control; hierarchical QP.

### 2. Operational Space Control

Khatib's 1987 operational-space formulation maps [robot dynamics](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E6%9C%BA%E5%99%A8%E4%BA%BA%E5%8A%A8%E5%8A%9B%E5%AD%A6&zhida_source=entity) from joint space to task space. Rather than merely computing a Jacobian, it constructs a task-space inertia: $\Lambda(q)=\left(JM^{-1}J^\top\right)^{-1}$

and handles inertia, [Coriolis forces](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E7%A7%91%E6%B0%8F%E5%8A%9B&zhida_source=entity), gravity, contact forces, and [null-space motion](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E9%9B%B6%E7%A9%BA%E9%97%B4%E8%BF%90%E5%8A%A8&zhida_source=entity) directly in task space. ([IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1087068?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) It answers the question: "what dynamics should the end-effector exhibit," not just "where should the end-effector go."

Many low-level capabilities of modern torque-controlled manipulators—including Cartesian inertia shaping, force control, and the dynamically consistent pseudoinverse—trace back to this line of work.

### 3. The French Task Function and Stack of Tasks

The task-function approach of Samson, Le Borgne, and Espiau formulates vision, geometry, and force tasks uniformly as [error functions](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E8%AF%AF%E5%B7%AE%E5%87%BD%E6%95%B0&zhida_source=entity), driven to convergence via feedback. Later, Chaumette, Mansard, and others at INRIA and LAAS extended this into sensor-based task sequencing and the Stack of Tasks. ([Stanford SearchWorks](https://searchworks.stanford.edu/view/677902?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The hierarchical quadratic programming framework of Escande, Mansard, and Wieber (2014) handles, under strict task priority:

-   Equality and [inequality constraints](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E4%B8%8D%E7%AD%89%E5%BC%8F%E7%BA%A6%E6%9D%9F&zhida_source=entity);
-   Joint, velocity, and [torque limits](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%8A%9B%E7%9F%A9%E9%99%90%E5%88%B6&zhida_source=entity);
-   [Contact constraints](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E6%8E%A5%E8%A7%A6%E7%BA%A6%E6%9D%9F&zhida_source=entity);
-   [Friction cones](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E6%91%A9%E6%93%A6%E9%94%A5&zhida_source=entity);
-   [Collision constraints](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=2&q=%E7%A2%B0%E6%92%9E%E7%BA%A6%E6%9D%9F&zhida_source=entity);
-   Multiple manipulation tasks.

This is stricter than a simple [weighted](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%8A%A0%E6%9D%83&zhida_source=entity) loss: lower-priority tasks cannot, in principle, violate higher-priority ones. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0278364914521306?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## IV. Force Control, [Compliance](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E9%A1%BA%E5%BA%94%E6%80%A7&zhida_source=entity), and Fine-Motion Planning

This is arguably the most valuable branch of classical manipulation for re-examination, yet the one most easily overlooked by modern papers.

### 1. Hybrid Position/Force Control

Raibert and Craig (1981) proposed assigning certain directions in task coordinates to position control and others to force control. For example, controlling position tangentially along a table surface while regulating contact force along the normal. ([ASME Digital Collection](https://asmedigitalcollection.asme.org/dynamicsystems/article/103/2/126/400298/Hybrid-Position-Force-Control-of-Manipulators?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The core idea is to partition the task space according to the [constraint surface](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E7%BA%A6%E6%9D%9F%E9%9D%A2&zhida_source=entity) into $S$ and $I-S$, selecting position and force control subspaces respectively. Its limitations are evident: it requires reasonably accurate task coordinates and constraint directions, and poorly handled mode switching can produce impacts or instability.

### 2. Impedance Control

Hogan's 1985 impedance control reframed the problem: instead of specifying position or force directly, it specifies the [mechanical impedance](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E6%9C%BA%E6%A2%B0%E9%98%BB%E6%8A%97&zhida_source=entity) the robot presents during contact with the environment. The typical target relationship is: $F_{\mathrm{ext}} = M_d\ddot e + D_d\dot e + K_de.$

This is why real robots, even with imprecise high-level trajectories, can often "wiggle in," "press down," or "follow a surface." Much of the contact robustness in modern learning systems in fact derives from low-level impedance and mechanical compliance. ([ASME Digital Collection](https://asmedigitalcollection.asme.org/dynamicsystems/article/107/1/1/400604/Impedance-Control-An-Approach-to-Manipulation-Part?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

[Germany](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%BE%B7%E5%9B%BD&zhida_source=entity)'s DLR subsequently developed a highly complete theoretical and hardware framework around lightweight arms, joint torque sensing, Cartesian impedance, and passivity. The work of Albu-Schäffer, Ott, Hirzinger, and colleagues is representative of this direction. ([DLR Electronic Library](https://elib.dlr.de/11848/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

### 3. Assembly and Fine Motion

Whitney's analysis of compliant assembly, and the 1984 fine-motion strategy synthesis of Lozano-Pérez, Mason, and Taylor, do not ask "how to generate a nice trajectory." Instead, they ask: given bounded geometric, sensing, and execution errors, what contact motions guarantee that the system will eventually reach the goal set?

The latter explicitly uses:

-   Position uncertainty sets;
-   Velocity cones;
-   Contact geometry;
-   Guarded motion;
-   Compliant motion;
-   Termination predicates

to synthesize action strategies with correctness conditions. ([ASME Digital Collection](https://asmedigitalcollection.asme.org/dynamicsystems/article/104/1/65/428529/Quasi-Static-Assembly-of-Compliantly-Supported?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Erdmann's 1986 backprojection method further computes backwards:

$$
\mathrm{Pre}(G,u) = \{x : \text{executing action } u \text{ necessarily leads into } G\}
$$

That is, it seeks a set of states such that, starting from any state within it, executing the action under bounded error reaches the goal. Donald, and later Latombe, Lazanas, and Shekhar, studied error detection, recovery, and planning under sensing/control uncertainty. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/027836498600500102?utm_source=chatgpt.com)). This line of work provides genuine **set-based robustness**, far stronger than "initialize randomly 20 times and succeed 17."

## V. Grasp Theory and Dexterous Manipulation

The core of classical grasping is not detecting a 2D grasp bounding box, but establishing the mechanical structure of the hand–object system. The multi-modality of this structure can be seen here:

[https://www.zhihu.com/question/588122312/answer/72170988777?share_code=EmjHwgun37J5&utm_psn=2065965954304042033](https://www.zhihu.com/question/588122312/answer/72170988777?share_code=EmjHwgun37J5&utm_psn=2065965954304042033)

### 1. Grasp Matrix and Internal Force

Salisbury and Craig (1982) studied the kinematics and force control of multifingered hands, distinguishing between:

-   Contact forces that change the net wrench on the object;
-   Internal forces that do not alter object motion but only increase grasp tightness.

The fundamental relationship is: $w = Gf$. Here, $f$ collects all contact forces, $G$ is the grasp matrix, and $w$ is the total wrench applied to the object. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/027836498200100102?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

### 2. Form Closure, Force Closure, and Wrench Space

Nguyen (1988) studied how to construct force-closure grasps; Ferrari and Canny (1992) formulated grasp quality as a geometric margin in grasp wrench space. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/027836498800700301?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Classical grasp quality is concerned with:

-   Whether arbitrary external forces can be resisted;
-   The minimal margin against external disturbances;
-   Friction cone approximation error;
-   Whether contact forces satisfy unilateral constraints;
-   Whether a sufficiently large [feasible region](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%8F%AF%E8%A1%8C%E5%9F%9F&zhida_source=entity) for internal forces exists.

This carries far more explicit physical meaning than "network-predicted grasp confidence."

### 3. Contact Kinematics

Montana (1988) used [differential geometry](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%BE%AE%E5%88%86%E5%87%A0%E4%BD%95&zhida_source=entity) to study rolling, sliding, and spinning contact motion between two surfaces. The state includes not only the object pose but also the contact coordinates on each surface. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/027836498800700302?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Classical mathematical problems in dexterous manipulation include:

-   [Nonholonomic constraints](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E9%9D%9E%E5%AE%8C%E6%95%B4%E7%BA%A6%E6%9D%9F&zhida_source=entity) of rolling contact;
-   Rolling/sliding mode transitions;
-   Hand–object [closed-chain kinematics](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E9%97%AD%E9%93%BE%E8%BF%90%E5%8A%A8&zhida_source=entity);
-   Contact Jacobians;
-   Internal force controllability;
-   Grasp stability;
-   Finger gaiting;
-   Regrasp.

Bicchi provided a [systematic analysis](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E7%B3%BB%E7%BB%9F%E5%88%86%E6%9E%90&zhida_source=entity) of grasp closure, dexterity, underactuation, and robust grasping; the 2000 survey by Bicchi and Kumar remains an excellent theoretical entry point. ([Centro Piaggio](https://www.centropiaggio.unipi.it/user/2/publications?f%5Bag%5D=L&o=desc&page=17&s=type&utm_source=chatgpt.com)). Mason's *Mechanics of Robotic Manipulation* is particularly important because it studies the "object motion processes" of grasping, pushing, rolling, sliding, throwing, and releasing—rather than only the manipulator itself. ([MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262133968/mechanics-of-robotic-manipulation/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## VI. Nonprehensile Manipulation and Contact Mechanics

Nonprehensile manipulation is the direct ancestor of today's contact-rich manipulation.

Mason (1986) systematically studied the mechanics and planning of planar pushing; Erdmann and Mason (1988) introduced sensorless manipulation, which actively exploits contact and environmental geometry to reduce state uncertainty. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/027836498600500303?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) A crucial idea here is that contact is not merely a disturbance to be overcome—it can itself serve as a mechanism for information acquisition and state contraction.

For example, pushing a part against a corner, even without precise visual measurements, can reduce the uncertainty of the final state.

Lynch and Mason (1996) connected stable pushing to nonholonomic constraints, controllability, and planning. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/027836499601500602?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) In contact simulation, Stewart and Trinkle (1996) developed an implicit time-stepping method that uses complementarity conditions to handle rigid bodies, Coulomb friction, simultaneous contacts, and impacts—a foundational contribution for what later became:

-   Complementarity-based contact dynamics;
-   Contact-implicit trajectory optimization;
-   Differentiable contact simulation;
-   Rigid-body time stepping.

([Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/%28SICI%291097-0207%2819960815%2939%3A15%3C2673%3A%3AAID-NME972%3E3.0.CO%3B2-I?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## VII. Manipulation Planning and [TAMP](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=TAMP&zhida_source=entity)

TAMP is not a concept that emerged only with large models. Its classical core is this: discrete task decisions alter the continuous feasible space, while continuous geometric feasibility can, in turn, invalidate discrete plans.

### 1\. Manipulation Graphs and Mode Manifolds

At LAAS in France, Alami, Siméon, and Laumond early on formulated manipulation as mode transitions between grasps and placements.

Alami, Siméon, and Laumond (1989/1990) studied manipulation planning under discrete grasping and placement modes; Siméon, Laumond, Cortés, and Sahbani (2004) later used probabilistic roadmaps to plan across grasp manifolds and placement manifolds. ([Inria HAL](https://inria.hal.science/hal-01309950/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The configuration space here is not an ordinary free space but a collection of manifolds of varying dimension:

-   The placement manifold where an object rests on a table;
-   The grasp manifold where the arm holds the object;
-   The closed-chain manifold of bimanual grasping;
-   The intersection manifold where multiple constraints hold simultaneously during handover.

The crux of manipulation planning is finding feasible intersection points between these manifolds.

### 2\. Hybrid Task-Motion Planning

Cambon, Alami, and Gravot (2009) more tightly integrated symbolic task planning with [geometric constraints](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%87%A0%E4%BD%95%E7%BA%A6%E6%9D%9F&zhida_source=entity); Hauser and Latombe (2010) approached motion across different constrained manifolds from the perspective of multi-modal planning. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0278364908097884?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Kaelbling and Lozano-Pérez (2011) proposed hierarchical TAMP, emphasizing hierarchical decomposition, delayed commitment, and on-demand geometric checks; they subsequently extended the problem to belief space, jointly reasoning about action, perception, and geometry under partial observability. ([IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/5980391?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Toussaint (2015) introduced Logic-Geometric Programming, which combines:

-   Logical mode sequences;
-   Kinematic constraints;
-   Contact constraints;
-   Continuous trajectory optimization

into a unified optimization framework. ([ACM Digital Library](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/2832415.2832517?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Therefore, the key to classical TAMP is not "getting a language model to output a pick/place sequence," but rather:

1.  Whether discrete operators correspond to actual geometrically feasible regions;
2.  Whether manifold intersections exist during mode transitions;
3.  Whether contact modes satisfy mechanical feasibility;
4.  Whether continuous trajectories can connect these intersections;
5.  Whether uncertainty can invalidate preconditions.

## VIII. Multicontact and Whole-Body Manipulation

Multicontact is often mistakenly seen as merely a branch of humanoid locomotion. Mathematically, however, it is much closer to **generalized manipulation**:

-   Foot-ground contact;
-   Hand bracing against a wall;
-   Knee or elbow support;
-   Pushing or pulling objects with both hands;
-   Leaning the body against the environment;
-   Grasping with one hand while supporting with the other.

All of these amount to "selecting contact modes and distributing wrenches subject to friction constraints."

### 1\. Contact Mode Planning

Bretl (2006) treated different limb contact combinations as distinct mode manifolds in free-climbing robot planning; Bretl and Lall (2008) further studied how to accurately determine static equilibrium and feasible centroidal regions under arbitrary contact and terrain conditions. ([SciSpace](https://scispace.com/papers/motion-planning-of-multi-limbed-robots-subject-to-2ybdybnwf4?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

### 2\. The French [Multicontact](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=multicontact&zhida_source=entity) Research Line

Bouyarmane, Kheddar, and colleagues at JRL/AIST-CNRS studied:

-   Multicontact stance generation;
-   Contact transition planning;
-   Joint manipulation and locomotion planning;
-   Potential-field-guided contact planning;
-   Multi-robot or multi-agent contact.

Representative works include multicontact acyclic planning, multiple-agent stance planning, and humanoid locomotion/manipulation step planning. ([LORIA](https://members.loria.fr/kbouyarmane/mcpc.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

### 3\. Contact Forces and Whole-Body QP

Once the modes are determined, an online solution is still required:

$$
\begin{aligned} M(q)\ddot q+h(q)&=S^\top\tau+J_c^\top\lambda, J_c\ddot q+\dot Jc\dot q=0, \\ \lambda&\in\mathcal K{\mathrm{friction}}, \\ \tau{\min}&\leq\tau\leq\tau{\max}. \end{aligned}
$$

This is then implemented hierarchically via HQP:

1.  Maintain dynamic and contact feasibility;
2.  Maintain balance;
3.  Execute the hand manipulation task;
4.  Adjust posture, avoid joint limits, and prevent collisions.

The HQP framework by Escande, Mansard, and Wieber is a major representative of this framework. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0278364914521306?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## IX. Trajectory Time Parameterization and Movement Primitives

### 1\. The Difference Between Paths and Trajectories

A geometric planner typically produces only a path (q(s)). Actual execution requires determining (s(t)) so that velocity, acceleration, and torque constraints are satisfied.

Bobrow, Dubowsky, and Gibson (1985) studied time-optimal control along a given path, a line of work that later evolved into various time-optimal path parameterization methods. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/027836498500400301?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

This part is often hidden in the trajectory generators of modern manipulation work, but it determines:

-   Whether torque limits are violated;
-   Whether sharp corners induce large accelerations;
-   Whether contact forces remain controllable;
-   Whether execution time is reasonable.

### 2\. Dynamical System Movement Primitives

DMPs strictly belong to learning from demonstration, but they do not follow today's data-driven paradigm. Ijspeert, Nakanishi, and Schaal represented actions as nonlinear dynamical systems with stable [attractors](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%90%B8%E5%BC%95%E5%AD%90&zhida_source=entity), using a small number of parameters to shape trajectory shape while preserving terminal [convergence properties](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E6%94%B6%E6%95%9B%E6%80%A7%E8%B4%A8&zhida_source=entity). ([IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1014739/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Billard and Calinon at EPFL, Dillmann at Karlsruhe, and Schaal and colleagues formed the early programming by demonstration lineage. Their work typically emphasizes:

-   Dynamical system stability;
-   Generalization in task coordinates;
-   Probabilistic models of trajectories;
-   Constraints and online modulation;
-   Combining human demonstrations with control structures,

rather than mapping directly from pixels to motor commands. ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-3-540-30301-5_60?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## Summary

### 1\. What "Robustness" Really Means in the Classical Literature

Classical manipulation encompasses at least five distinct notions of robustness.

1.  Set-based robustness: Given bounds on sensing and control errors, prove that a certain set of initial states necessarily reaches a target set. Representative work includes fine-motion planning, preimage/backprojection, and uncertainty planning. ([Johns Hopkins University](https://pure.johnshopkins.edu/en/publications/automatic-synthesis-of-fine-motion-strategies-for-robots-8/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))
2.  Stability and passivity: Using tools such as Lyapunov analysis, passivity, and impedance control, guarantee that the robot does not inject unbounded energy when in contact with an unknown passive environment. Representative work includes the impedance/passivity lines of Hogan and DLR. ([ASME Digital Collection](https://asmedigitalcollection.asme.org/dynamicsystems/article/107/1/1/400604/Impedance-Control-An-Approach-to-Manipulation-Part?utm_source=chatgpt.com))
3.  Grasp wrench margins: Compute the minimal resistance to external disturbances using the grasp wrench space or friction cones. Representative work includes Nguyen, Ferrari–Canny, and Bicchi. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/027836498800700301?utm_source=chatgpt.com))
4.  Search completeness: The probabilistic completeness of PRM/RRT shows that as samples increase, the probability of finding an existing path approaches one; it does not guarantee success under execution errors. ([Kavraki Lab](https://www.kavrakilab.org/publications/kavraki-svestka1996probabilistic-roadmaps-for.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com))
5.  Contact feasibility and stability margins: Including friction cones, ZMP/CoP, support polygons, centroidal wrenches, static-equilibrium regions, and contact-force feasibility. This is the central focus of Bretl, Kheddar, Wieber, Mansard, and others. ([ACM Digital Library](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1109/TRO.2008.2001360?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Therefore, simply reporting "success rate" collapses five fundamentally different notions into a single number.

### 2\. Why You Might Think Older Manipulation Literature Is Scarce

1.  The keywords have changed: Many classical works do not even contain “robot manipulation” in their titles. Useful search terms include: `fine motion planning, compliant motion, robot assembly, mechanics of manipulation, contact kinematics, force closure, grasp wrench space, sensorless manipulation, nonprehensile manipulation, manipulation planning, multi-modal motion planning, task function approach, operational space control, task frame formalism, whole-body control, multicontact planning`. Searching only for `manipulation policy` or `robot manipulation planning` will naturally flood the results with recent learning papers.
2.  Manipulation has no (or only a very difficult) single low-dimensional simplification: ZMP can compress locomotion into a centroid and support-region problem under certain assumptions; HZD can construct hybrid zero dynamics around [periodic orbits](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%91%A8%E6%9C%9F%E8%BD%A8%E9%81%93&zhida_source=entity). Manipulation, however, simultaneously involves: object pose + finger state + contact positions + contact modes + friction + environment geometry + grasp topology. When contacts are established or broken, the system dimension and constraint structure both change. It is therefore difficult to form a single unifying paradigm like ZMP that covers most of the problem.
3.  Classical results are distributed across books and long-standing research programs: Some truly important knowledge is not concentrated in the benchmark venues of the time but in monographs, handbooks, and long-term laboratory research systems—for example, Latombe's motion planning, Laumond's European planning school, Mason's manipulation mechanics, and the control and contact research at DLR, LAAS, INRIA, Pisa, and KU Leuven. ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4615-4022-9_1?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

### 3\. Core Values

In my view, what is truly worth restoring is not "abandoning learning entirely and returning to exact models," but rather the three fundamental principles of classical manipulation:

1.  Contact modes must be treated as [system structure](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E7%B3%BB%E7%BB%9F%E7%BB%93%E6%9E%84&zhida_source=entity), not as latent variables in data.
2.  Robustness should correspond to well-defined sets, stability margins, wrench margins, or probabilistic guarantees.
3.  High-level action generation and low-level feedback control must be evaluated jointly; one cannot attribute the contributions of an impedance controller to a vision policy.

The problems with classical methods are equally real: they rely on accurate geometry, friction, and contact models; they scale poorly when faced with visual semantics and large-scale open [world](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E4%B8%96%E7%95%8C%E6%97%B6&zhida_source=entity) scenarios; and discrete contact-mode combinations quickly explode combinatorially. But at minimum, they clearly articulate **why the system succeeds, where the failure boundaries are, and which layer guarantees which property**.

If we are to establish a manipulation research agenda that goes beyond current VLAs, I believe the most promising combination is not "ever-larger [datasets](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=280113316&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E6%95%B0%E6%8D%AE%E9%9B%86&zhida_source=entity)," but rather:

$$
\boxed{ \text{contact-mode reasoning} + \text{differentiable/complementarity contact mechanics} + \text{set-based uncertainty} + \text{hierarchical feedback control} + \text{learned perception and model residuals} }
$$

That is, let learning take over the parts where classical methods are genuinely weak, rather than using a single policy network to obscure geometry, mechanics, control, and planning all at once.

### 5\. The European Lineage, Especially Worth Noting

Your intuition is correct. There is a long and strong tradition in European manipulation research that runs from "formal models → numerical algorithms → real systems." This does not mean the classical American work is less solid; Mason, Khatib, Lozano-Pérez, Latombe, and Salisbury are equally foundational to the entire field.

1.  France: LAAS-CNRS, INRIA, JRL

Core themes include: task-function; sensor-based control; Stack of Tasks; manipulation graph; TAMP; multicontact; whole-body HQP. Laumond's edited volume *Robot Motion Planning and Control* itself originated from the European PROMotion project; the work of Alami, Siméon, Mansard, Wieber, Kheddar, and others forms a remarkably coherent thread. ([ENS](https://www.di.ens.fr/jean-paul.laumond/promotion/book.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

2\. Italy: Pisa / Centro Piaggio

The Bicchi school focuses on: grasp closure; dexterous manipulation; underactuation; soft synergies; robust grasping; tactile and haptic interaction. Its distinguishing feature is addressing grasping maps, controllability, contact mechanics, and mechanism design simultaneously. ([Centro Piaggio](https://www.centropiaggio.unipi.it/user/2/publications?f%5Bag%5D=L&o=desc&page=17&s=type&utm_source=chatgpt.com))

3\. Germany: DLR

DLR's core contributions are: torque-controlled lightweight robots; joint-torque sensing; Cartesian impedance; passivity; flexible-joint control; interaction safety. Many of the low-level control structures in modern high-performance manipulator arms find clear origins in this lineage. ([Electronic Library](https://elib.dlr.de/11848/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

4\. Belgium: KU Leuven

De Schutter, Bruyninckx, and colleagues developed the Task Frame Formalism and constraint-based task specification, aiming to describe contact tasks in a clear geometric and constraint language rather than hand-writing a controller for every task. ([IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel4/70/11078/00508440.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

5\. The PbD Lineage in Switzerland and the Franco-German Border Region

EPFL LASA, Karlsruhe, and other institutions studied programming by demonstration, probabilistic motion representations, DMPs, and stable dynamical systems at an early stage. This lineage is a very valuable middle ground between model-based control and learning, well worth revisiting. ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-3-540-30301-5_60?utm_source=chatgpt.com))
