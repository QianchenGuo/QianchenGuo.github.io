## [Stochastic System Control Theory](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=245917796&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E9%9A%8F%E6%9C%BA%E7%B3%BB%E7%BB%9F%E6%8E%A7%E5%88%B6%E7%90%86%E8%AE%BA&zhida_source=entity)

The result of identifying a system based on data acquired from actual processes is typically a model whose parameters are given only by the mean and [covariance](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=245917796&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%8D%8F%E6%96%B9%E5%B7%AE&zhida_source=entity). In such cases, when uncertainty is considered in stochastic systems, the [optimal control](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=245917796&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E6%9C%80%E4%BC%98%E6%8E%A7%E5%88%B6&zhida_source=entity) strategy is a cautious strategy. A control strategy derived using only the mean of [random variables](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=245917796&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E9%9A%8F%E6%9C%BA%E5%8F%98%E9%87%8F&zhida_source=entity) while ignoring uncertainty is called a certainty-equivalent control strategy.

Thanks to the work of J.M. Bismut, W.H. Fleming, and S. Peng, among others, the [optimal control theory](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=245917796&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E6%9C%80%E4%BC%98%E6%8E%A7%E5%88%B6%E7%90%86%E8%AE%BA&zhida_source=entity) for stochastic finite-dimensional systems has been thoroughly studied. These works have broad applications in [financial mathematics](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=245917796&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E9%87%91%E8%9E%8D%E6%95%B0%E5%AD%A6&zhida_source=entity), where S. Peng is Academician [Peng Shige](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=245917796&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%BD%AD%E5%AE%9E%E6%88%88&zhida_source=entity).

However, the controllability theory for stochastic systems lags far behind; even at the time of the lecture, there was no universally accepted definition of stochastic controllability.

Below, we consider a complete [probability space](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=245917796&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E6%A6%82%E7%8E%87%E7%A9%BA%E9%97%B4&zhida_source=entity) $(Ω,F,{\left\{F_{t}\right\}_{t≥0}},P)$ endowed with a filtration satisfying the usual conditions, together with a one-dimensional standard Brownian motion $\left\{B(t)\right\}_{t≥0}$ on it.

These are all analogous to the finite-dimensional case. How, then, do we characterize the optimal pair

$$
\left( \bar{x} \left( \cdot \right) ,\bar{u} \left( \cdot \right)\right)
$$

? That is, can we obtain a corresponding Pontryagin-type maximum principle?

1\. For the case $dimH<\infty$, see S. Peng (1990);

2\. For cases where the [diffusion term](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=245917796&content_type=Article&match_order=2&q=%E6%89%A9%E6%95%A3%E9%A1%B9&zhida_source=entity) does not contain the control, or the control set is convex, see A. Bensoussan (1983), Y. Hu and S. Peng (1992), B. Øksendal (2005), V. V. Anh, W. Grecksch and J. Yong (2010), A. Al-Hussein (2010, 2011), among others;

3\. For cases where the diffusion term contains the control and the control set is nonconvex, see X. Y. Zhou (1993) (linear problems), S. Tang and [X. Li](http://X.Li) (1994) (rather special results).

The problem is far more complex than in the finite-dimensional setting. The main difficulty lies in the following: in the finite-dimensional case we defined an $\mathbb R^{n\times n}-$matrix-valued backward stochastic differential equation as the adjoint equation, but in the [infinite-dimensional](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=245917796&content_type=Article&match_order=3&q=%E6%97%A0%E7%A9%B7%E7%BB%B4&zhida_source=entity) case, how do we define the solution of an operator-valued backward stochastic differential equation?

1\. When $H=\mathbb R^{n}$, an $\mathbb R^{n\times n}-$matrix-valued backward stochastic differential equation can be viewed as an $\mathbb R^{n^2}$-([vector](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=245917796&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%90%91%E9%87%8F&zhida_source=entity))-valued backward stochastic differential equation;

2\. When $dimH=\infty$, $\mathcal{L}\left( H \right)$ is a [Banach space](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=245917796&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=Banach%E7%A9%BA%E9%97%B4&zhida_source=entity) under the usual operator topology, but it is no longer reflexive, nor separable, and hence certainly not a [Hilbert space](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=245917796&content_type=Article&match_order=2&q=Hilbert%E7%A9%BA%E9%97%B4&zhida_source=entity).

In general Banach spaces, no satisfactory stochastic integration theory is yet available. For instance, how should one define the Itô integral $\int_{0}^{T}Q\left( s \right)dw\left( s \right)$, where $Q\left( \cdot \right)$ is an operator-valued process? Currently, [UMD-type](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=245917796&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=UMD%E5%9E%8B&zhida_source=entity) Banach spaces have a corresponding theory, but whenever a Banach space is of UMD type, it must be reflexive; hence $\mathcal{L}\left( H \right)$ cannot be UMD. Therefore, new ideas are needed to complete this part of the theory. (Recent progress from the lecture notes, posted on arXiv on 2012.4.15:)

Q. Lü, X. Zhang, *General Pontryagin-Type Stochastic Maximum Principle and Backward Stochastic Evolution Equations in Infinite Dimensions*, SpringerBriefs in Mathematics, Springer, 2014, to appear.

Subsequently, some of the results were extended or improved:

1\. K. Du and Q. Meng, A maximum principle for optimal control of stochastic evolution equations, *SIAM J. Control Optim.*, 51 (2013), 4343–4362.

2\. M. Fuhrman, Y. Hu and G. Tessitore, Stochastic maximum principle for optimal control of SPDEs, *Appl. Math. Optim.*, 68 (2013), 181–217.
