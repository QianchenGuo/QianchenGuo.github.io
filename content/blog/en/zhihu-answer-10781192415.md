You are absolutely right. For instance, consider a limit cycle in the [phase plane](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=695271905&content_type=Answer&match_order=1&q=%E7%9B%B8%E5%B9%B3%E9%9D%A2&zhida_source=entity) \((x, \dot{x})\) that forms a circle centered at the origin. In this case, \(x(t)\) is exactly a [sinusoidal function](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=695271905&content_type=Answer&match_order=1&q=%E6%AD%A3%E5%BC%A6%E5%9E%8B%E5%87%BD%E6%95%B0&zhida_source=entity) \(A \sin(\omega(t) t + \phi)\). Note that the state \(x\) does not necessarily evolve at a uniform speed along the limit cycle, so \(\omega\) is actually a function of \(t\). Clearly, \(A\), the radius of the circle, is the amplitude; when \(\dot{x} = dx/dt = 0\), \(x\) reaches its maximum value \(A\).
