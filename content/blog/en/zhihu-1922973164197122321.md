## **[Decision and Planning for Mobile Intelligent Agents](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=259718598&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E7%A7%BB%E5%8A%A8%E6%99%BA%E8%83%BD%E4%BD%93%E5%86%B3%E7%AD%96%E8%A7%84%E5%88%92&zhida_source=entity): the rising sea**

[https://www.zhihu.com/question/1937525716888582111/answer/1937532644624662647?share_code=1b8GecWALtYoe&utm_psn=1955564680216049490](https://www.zhihu.com/question/1937525716888582111/answer/1937532644624662647?share_code=1b8GecWALtYoe&utm_psn=1955564680216049490)

Decision and planning is a new theoretical framework that emerges naturally from [control theory](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=259718598&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E6%8E%A7%E5%88%B6%E7%90%86%E8%AE%BA&zhida_source=entity) when fully automated systems are placed in more complex environments. This series focuses mostly on "motion"-related contexts, primarily addressing decision-making and planning in the movement of mobile intelligent agents (also known as embodied intelligence). Its theoretical foundations are rooted in (and encompass) predictive control, optimal control and its numerical methods, [differential games](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=259718598&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%BE%AE%E5%88%86%E5%8D%9A%E5%BC%88&zhida_source=entity), among other branches. Its [mathematical foundations](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=259718598&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E6%95%B0%E5%AD%A6%E5%9F%BA%E7%A1%80&zhida_source=entity) span feedback control theory, [optimal control theory](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=259718598&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E6%9C%80%E4%BC%98%E6%8E%A7%E5%88%B6%E7%90%86%E8%AE%BA&zhida_source=entity), numerical methods for [differential equations](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=259718598&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%BE%AE%E5%88%86%E6%96%B9%E7%A8%8B&zhida_source=entity), and numerical optimization. Of course, "non-motion" decision and planning theory is not detached from these either.

On the one hand, as a master's student who has lost his way in the field of motion planning, this series serves as a **memory aid and compilation**, a supplement to classic materials, and a way to sort through my own scattered thoughts, unconstrained by the confines of any particular course or textbook. On the other hand, I hope to conduct a **systematic "course correction"** for the chaos in the Chinese internet's motion planning and related fields (including buzzwords like reinforcement learning, imitation learning, behavior cloning, end-to-end, VLA, and [world models](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=259718598&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E4%B8%96%E7%95%8C%E6%A8%A1%E5%9E%8B&zhida_source=entity)), and to bridge the misunderstandings and disputes among different communities. Furthermore, for readers who have encountered some planning algorithms before, this series will reveal many old friends—such as search-based and sampling-based methods, as well as polynomial trajectory optimization approaches—reappearing here as special cases.

As is well known, the premise of solving a problem scientifically is to define it well. Once well-defined, trivial problems lack essential difficulties; they are merely superficially complex. Nontrivial problems, by contrast, possess a [core](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=259718598&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%86%85%E6%A0%B8&zhida_source=entity) that touches the essence, along with other nontrivial subproblems. Correspondingly, [research work](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=259718598&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E7%A7%91%E7%A0%94%E5%B7%A5%E4%BD%9C&zhida_source=entity) generally falls into two categories: one circles around the surface, solving scattered problems; the other confronts the essence head-on, attempting to advance human understanding and capability in dealing with the core through various techniques. The articles I present below strive to dissect essential problems and, through deeper understanding, to push forward the solution of [hard problems](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=259718598&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=hard+problem&zhida_source=entity). Moreover, I uphold a systemic mindset, habitually mapping out, from a macro perspective, the existing approaches to hard problems. Through this series, I hope to leverage my strength in systematization and, **from an elevated viewpoint and a grand unified perspective**, appreciate motion planning—striving (standing on the shoulders of various giants) to build a framework that is unprecedented and world-leading [\[1\]](#ref_1). There are also resources like [Optimal Control (Useful resources for OCP)](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/395525975?share_code=7iDpk1LOh2LQ&utm_psn=1979983589694277392) that summarize optimal control and numerical methods quite well, but unfortunately, a more cohesive and unified framework as envisioned here has yet to appear.

However, given my limited time, the articles may inevitably seem somewhat pieced together. Most articles may not list many formulas and will be text-dominated (or even perpetually postponed). Some may initially be reposts of answers or articles from other experts. Nonetheless, private discussions are welcome, and I ask for your indulgence.

### II. Materials on Nonlinear System Control

Back in my junior year, I first created an article on nonlinear control and [geometric control](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=259718598&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%87%A0%E4%BD%95%E6%8E%A7%E5%88%B6&zhida_source=entity), intending to organize the existing resources on Zhihu. This is genuinely useful; cost design in motion planning (known as reward shaping in reinforcement learning) fundamentally relies on control theory.

### III. Tasks, Planning, and Intelligence

This article dissects the very definition of motion planning and planning in general, deriving broad concepts such as [static planning](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=259718598&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E9%9D%99%E6%80%81%E8%A7%84%E5%88%92&zhida_source=entity) and generalized dynamic planning. It systematically elaborates on how current motion planning paradigms address these core difficulties.

Moreover, the article posits that the fundamental path to achieving intelligence (arguably without equal) is planning. Motion planning addresses the motion level, but the key reasoning and thinking of intelligence must also be solved by planning, termed "task-level planning." This level exhibits higher complexity in its fundamental challenges and is difficult to handle well due to our limited understanding of tasks. Upon reflection, I believe one viable route is:

-   Discovering richer structure within these task problems, even achieving a complete state formulation for these more complex problems;
-   Crafting more sophisticated task-level costs, which often relies on nonlinear control theory;
-   Employing more techniques to make planning approximately solvable.

Consequently, in "Mobile Intelligent Agent Decision and Planning: The Rising Sea," I proposed a comprehensive methodology following these lines, particularly focusing on approximation solution theory.

### IV. Optimal Sequential Decision, Statistical Inference, and Planning

In this article, I focus on generalizing standard motion planning to [probability distributions](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=259718598&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E6%A6%82%E7%8E%87%E5%88%86%E5%B8%83&zhida_source=entity) to achieve better performance in [multi-modal](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=259718598&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%A4%9A%E6%A8%A1%E6%80%81&zhida_source=entity) problems or probabilistic systems. Two core principles are emphasized:

-   Regardless of whether the system is Markovian or non-Markovian, deterministic or stochastic, sparsity is the most critical element for real-time motion planning when no more complex structures (robustness, task-level, etc.) are involved. In deterministic systems, careful problem formulation reveals sparsity through the Riccati iteration enabled by the Markov property; stochastic systems are more flexible, allowing the sparsity of the problem to be converted into a graph structure using probabilistic [graphical models](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=259718598&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E5%9B%BE%E6%A8%A1%E5%9E%8B&zhida_source=entity), which naturally handles non-Markovian systems as well.
-   For problems with complex costs that are difficult to optimize, one can leverage advances in zeroth-order optimization to effectively tackle high-dimensional, complex cost functions. The motion planning problem, as an optimal sequential problem, then becomes a statistical inference problem framed as "message passing" on a factor graph. The variational inference framework provides a solid approximation for message passing algorithms.

Finally, the article notes that such techniques can even couple the front end and back end, making this an extremely powerful framework.

### I. Shenlan College: Mobile Robot Motion Planning

"Mobile Robot Motion Planning" is generally introductory, consistent with Shenlan College's usual style – not going deep enough to alienate audiences, yet still niche.

-   It concisely covers the "three pillars" of motion planning: search, sampling, and optimization.
-   Since Fei Gao's group primarily works on drones, and this course is taught entirely by his group members, the overall framework leans heavily toward aerial vehicles. The [trajectory planning](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=259718598&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E8%BD%A8%E8%BF%B9%E8%A7%84%E5%88%92&zhida_source=entity) sections particularly emphasize polynomial trajectory optimization (a signature topic of Gao's group).

In practice, this presentation is quite biased (approaching a course on path planning plus polynomial trajectory optimization). It overlooks much of what motion planning entails, and the content for drones versus autonomous vehicles differs considerably. As an introduction, it is passable, if unremarkable.

### II. LaValle's "Planning Algorithms"

-   It is systematic, in-depth, and comprehensive, and is a classic text in motion planning (one of the few with a Chinese translation). It is the nominal reference book for Fei Gao's mobile robot motion planning course (though the actual course content diverges significantly from the book).
-   However, due to its publication date, the material is somewhat dated. It suits those with strong intuition to grasp the ideas of motion planning, or those learning under guidance, but it is less suitable as a self-study resource. This weakness is especially pronounced now that newer frameworks for motion planning theory (i.e., the aforementioned "Mobile Intelligent Agent Decision and Planning: The Rising Sea") have emerged.
-   Additionally, some parts of the Chinese translation are of mediocre quality.

In summary, it is worth selectively reading.

### III. CMU 16-745

I find Zach's course on optimal control and reinforcement learning difficult to evaluate. As a graduate-level course, I suspect its widespread acclaim stems from its simplicity. I genuinely question his depth of understanding regarding numerical OCP. I know someone who works specifically on numerical OCP and has consulted Zach in person about numerical MPC details; the responses seemed superficial and general, lacking the sense of "this person is a true expert."

In the course, the shooting method was dismissed as merely indirect [shooting](https://zhida.zhihu.com/search?content_id=259718598&content_type=Article&match_order=1&q=%E6%89%93%E9%9D%B6%E6%B3%95&zhida_source=entity), characterized simply as solving the boundary value problem from Pontryagin's principle – labeled as old, unstable, and impractical, followed by "we won't dwell on it." The direct methods were covered only briefly, touching on SCP and collocation, while DDP and iLQR received the most attention. This has led to a significant misconception across the Chinese internet, where many parrot the idea that beyond Pontryagin, there are only two classes of methods: DDP-type and direct collocation. Even some (Zhihu) "authorities" propagate this. This is the most damaging impact – polluting the environment.

On the positive side, the course covers a broad range of topics, touching on various areas, which is genuinely helpful for domestic students to broaden their horizons. Compared to the often inadequate domestic curricula, it could be considered fine dining.
